    07Method and limits

    What this can and can't say.

    • Funding and need: funding per appeal from OCHA FTS; people in need from HDX HAPI (intersectoral, national level). Funding is for the current year.
    • Score is relative: 100 means the most neglected of the crises compared, not that anything is "solved" at 0. Weights are fixed at 40/40/20, and a robustness check re-ran the ranking under six other weightings.
    • Regional response plans (refugee-hosting countries) are excluded because per-country funding is unreliable there.
    • Older needs figures are flagged: dollars per person mixes two years, so the rank is indicative.
    • Media is a sample: English-language articles with humanitarian framing from GDELT, at most 250 per crisis, from roughly the last month. It shows the mix of coverage, not the total amount.
    • "State-linked" means an outlet owned, funded or controlled by a government. That describes ownership, not accuracy, and the list is short, so the shares are a floor.
    • Propaganda cannot be measured from a headline count. Whether a specific article misleads needs reading it, so this page shows who is speaking and leaves that judgement to case studies.
    • Correlation is not cause: research on disaster relief finds that media attention can shape who gets help, but this page does not claim that coverage causes a country's funding level.
